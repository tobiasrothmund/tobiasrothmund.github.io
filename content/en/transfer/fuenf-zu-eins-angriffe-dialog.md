Cultural institutions and artists are increasingly targeted by anti-democratic actors. In "fünf zu eins", the online magazine of the German Federal Cultural Foundation – which twice a year brings together five positions from arts and culture on a question of our time – Tobias Rothmund puts these attacks into psychological perspective and offers guidance on how to respond.

**Key ideas**

- **Attacks are often symbolic:** They are aimed less at specific people or works than at what these stand for. This explains why people speak out without even knowing those they attack.
- **A campaign is not the audience:** Ideologically motivated campaigns are often pseudo-communication. They need to be distinguished from people who are critical but remain open to conversation.
- **Use dialogue deliberately:** Rather than a blanket defensive stance, it pays to design dialogue formats specifically for those who can be reached – and to adapt one's own communicative stance to the context.
- **Set your own images:** Cultural institutions should not only react to attacks but actively bring positive images and narratives into the public sphere, and ground their convictions in dialogue rather than taking them for granted.
