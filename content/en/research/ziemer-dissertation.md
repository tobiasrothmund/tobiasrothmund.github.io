This dissertation examines how psychological interventions can address misinformation and ideological bias. It aims to systematise and advance the current state of intervention research on misinformation and ideological bias by (1) providing a systematic overview of empirically tested interventions against misinformation, (2) highlighting the central role of social identity and ideology in motivated reasoning when evaluating misinformation and scientific evidence, and (3) demonstrating the effectiveness of selected psychological intervention strategies against motivated reasoning in the context of current socio-political issues.

Study 1 was a systematic literature review of 176 research papers on empirically tested psychological interventions against misinformation. The results showed that most studies focused on fact-checking, were only weakly grounded in basic psychological research and rarely addressed motivated reasoning.

Study 2 developed a text-based, strategic inoculation intervention against pro-Russian disinformation related to Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine, and examined social identity as a key vulnerability factor for motivated reasoning. Strong identification with Russia significantly increased susceptibility to Russian disinformation. However, the inoculation intervention reduced the perceived credibility of disinformation and also influenced war-related attitudes – regardless of the degree of identification with Russia.

Study 3 examined the effect of a feedback intervention on reducing ideological bias in the evaluation of scientific evidence. Feedback significantly reduced ideological bias among both left-leaning and conservative individuals. Adding a social-norm nudge, however, did not reduce ideological bias any further.

The findings of this cumulative dissertation deepen our understanding of the emerging, interdisciplinary field of interventions against misinformation and ideological bias. The thesis critically evaluates the strengths and weaknesses of existing interventions and provides evidence on effective countermeasures in a current socio-political context. By examining the central role of social identity and ideology in information processing, introducing the concept of ideological bias and testing combinations of interventions, it contributes to the theoretical development of the field. It also underlines the need for scalable, practice-oriented solutions to strengthen resilience to misinformation and ideological bias at both the individual and the societal level.
