The dissertation examines how psychological interventions can address misinformation and ideological bias. It systematises the state of research, highlights the role of social identity and ideology in motivated reasoning and tests selected intervention strategies in the context of current socio-political issues:

1. **Systematic overview:** A review of 176 studies on empirically tested interventions against misinformation shows that research relies mainly on fact-checking, is weakly grounded in theory and rarely addresses motivated reasoning.
2. **Inoculation against disinformation:** A text-based inoculation intervention against pro-Russian disinformation about the war of aggression against Ukraine reduced the perceived credibility of disinformation and influenced war-related attitudes – even among people who strongly identify with Russia and are therefore particularly susceptible.
3. **Feedback against ideological bias:** Feedback reduced ideologically biased evaluations of scientific evidence among both left-leaning and conservative individuals; an additional social-norm nudge had no further effect.

The cumulative dissertation thus contributes to the theoretical development of the field and underlines the need for scalable, practice-oriented solutions to strengthen resilience to misinformation and ideological bias.
