The COVID-19 pandemic showed how strongly the management of societal crises depends on people accepting scientific assessments and supporting public health measures. The project examined which social attitudes and personal characteristics explain whether people support protective measures or downplay and deny the pandemic – and to what extent pandemic denial can be understood as a communicative phenomenon.

The project combined two approaches:

1. **International multi-lab study:** As part of the *International Collaboration on Social & Moral Psychology of COVID-19* (ICSMP COVID-19), a consortium of more than 250 researchers surveyed over 50,000 people in almost 70 countries between April and June 2020 on attitudes and behaviour during the pandemic, identity, political ideology, moral beliefs, personality and well-being. The dataset is openly available to the research community. A key finding: people who identify more strongly with their nation showed greater support for public health policies and engaged more in protective behaviours such as physical distancing and hygiene. Machine learning methods were also used to examine which characteristics best predict attitudes and behaviour during the pandemic.
2. **Pandemic denial in Germany:** In spring 2020, a quota sample of the German public (N = 1,575) and virologists and epidemiologists (N = 128) rated the same claims about the virus. Around three quarters of the public largely agreed with the experts. Two groups – the **"Doubtful"** (19%) and the **"Dismissive"** (8%) – deviated markedly. They were characterised by **conspiracy thinking**, **less willingness to engage with complexity**, **right-wing political attitudes** and collective narcissism, and relied mainly on **social media, messenger services and alternative online channels** rather than established news media. The Dismissive also complied far less with distancing and hygiene rules.

The findings show that pandemic denial is closely linked to political identities, cognitive styles and media use – and that science communication in times of crisis should take these factors into account.
