Individual differences in general attitudes towards politics (political trust, political self-efficacy, political interest, willingness to participate, etc.) play a special role for democracy and have long been studied. A wide variety of constructs exists to describe these individual differences. This dissertation examines their underlying dimensional structure and proposes two higher-order dimensions – general political trust and general political involvement – understood as basic tendencies of thinking and feeling towards politics. In three journal articles, the dissertation examines the stability, generalisability and personality correlates of these higher-order tendencies.

*(Adapted from the dissertation, Bromme, 2021)*
